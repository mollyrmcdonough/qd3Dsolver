# III-V parameter set — full audit sheet

Generated by `scripts/dump_sb_parameters.py`, reading `materials.py` directly.
Every value below is what the code actually uses. Nothing is retyped.

Table evaluated at **T = 0 K**. Two quantities move with temperature:
the gaps (Varshni) and the lattice constants, and therefore every misfit and every strain.

## Read this first: the two conventions that decide whether a number is 'wrong'

**1. Sign convention for `a_v`.** This package applies

```
    E_c = E_c(unstrained) + a_c * Tr(eps)
    E_v = E_v(unstrained) - a_v * Tr(eps)        <-- MINUS
```

which is Pryor's convention, in which the hydrostatic **gap** deformation potential is

```
    a_gap = a_c + a_v
```

**Vurgaftman, Meyer and Ram-Mohan (2001) use this same convention**, and say so explicitly
— their `a_v` is negative for all four binaries here, and the gap potential is the SUM.
Since every number below is transcribed from their tables, no conversion is applied
anywhere and none is needed.

The commoner tabulation (Van de Walle) has `E_v` shifting by `+a_v*Tr(eps)`, quotes `a_v`
positive, and forms `a_gap = a_c - a_v`. **The two differ by 2\*a_v.** If you check `a_v`
against a source in that convention, convert first: `a_v(ours) = -a_v(theirs)`.
`qdsolver_core.MATERIALS` is in that other convention and is deliberately not migrated,
because the validated Pryor InAs/GaAs reproductions read it; do not mix the two.

The quantity that is convention-independent, and therefore the one to check, is `a_gap`.
It is given for every material below.

**2. Units.** Energies eV, elastic constants GPa, lattice constants Angstrom, `e14` C/m^2.
The workbook's elastic-constant rows are labelled GPa but tabulated in 10^9 dyne/cm^2
(GaAs `c11 = 1221` is 122.1 GPa); they are stored here in GPa. This supersedes an earlier
source, aestimo's `database.py`, which stored InAs `C11 = 8.329` (10^11 dyne/cm^2) beside
InSb `68.47` (GPa) — two units in one table, which made aestimo read GaSb and InSb 10x too
stiff. Nothing here imports that file.

## The alloys

Two, both parameterised by `x` = the InSb fraction. Interpolation is
`P(x) = x*P_A + (1-x)*P_B - C*x*(1-x)`, with `C` from
`bowingparameters_vurgaftman_2001.xlsx` and `C = 0` — linear — for every key not listed
there. That includes the Luttinger parameters, the deformation potentials, the elastic
constants, `VBO`, and the lattice constant (Vegard's law).

| alloy | endpoints | bowed keys |
|---|---|---|
| `InGaSb` | x=0 → GaSb, x=1 → InSb | `Eg` 0.415, `Eg_X` 0.33, `Eg_L` 0.4, `delta_so` 0.1, `m_e` 0.0092, `F` -6.84 |
| `InAsSb` | x=0 → InAs, x=1 → InSb | `Eg` 0.67, `Eg_X` 0.6, `Eg_L` 0.6, `delta_so` 1.2, `m_e` 0.035 |

`InAsSb`'s two large bowings are not typos. `Eg` bowing of 0.67 eV drives the gap to a
minimum **below either parent** at midcomposition, which is why that alloy is used for
long-wavelength infrared; `delta_so` bowing of 1.2 eV pulls the split-off from a 0.6 eV
linear value down to 0.30 eV there.

**The alloy electron mass is not well determined.** Bowing `m_e` directly and bowing the
ingredients of the Kane expression are two different prescriptions, and the review's
recommended values are not consistent between them: the strong `Eg` bowing pulls the
derived mass down faster than the tabulated `m_e` bowing does, by 9–16% depending on
composition. `electron_mass()` stays authoritative because the eight-band Hamiltonian is
assembled from `Ep`, `F`, `Eg` and `delta_so` and has no other option. Read the spread as
the real uncertainty — and note it lands squarely on the confinement threshold, which
goes as `1/m`.

## Temperature: the gaps, the lattice, and a check that picks a winner

Two things move with `T`, and `materials.set_temperature(T)` moves both:

```
    Eg(T) = Eg(0) - alpha*T^2 / (T + beta)         Varshni
    a0(T) = a0(300 K) + da/dT * (T - 300)          linear
```

The lattice constant is the one that is easy to forget and hard to notice. It is the
**only** thing that sets the misfit, and the misfit drives every strain result:

| | 0 K | 77 K | 300 K |
|---|---|---|---|
| InSb-on-InAs misfit (%) | -6.4752 | -6.4814 | -6.4991 |
| `Eg` InAs (eV) | 0.4170 | 0.4074 | 0.3538 |
| `Eg` InSb (eV) | 0.2350 | 0.2273 | 0.1737 |
| derived `m_e` InSb | 0.01350 | 0.01310 | 0.01022 |

The misfit moves only 0.4% of itself between 0 K and 300 K — worth about 1.5 meV on a
converged InSb dot gap — but mixing temperatures is how this project got into trouble
once already, in the other direction: the GaSb offset was derived from a 0 K table while
the gaps in force were ~300 K.

### The legacy `database` gaps, and why they lost

| | InAs | GaSb | InSb |
|---|---|---|---|
| `database` (~300 K, the OLD default) | 0.400 | 0.726 | 0.174 |
| **Varshni at T = 0 K (current default)** | **0.417** | **0.812** | **0.235** |
| Varshni `alpha` (eV/K) | 2.760e-04 | 4.170e-04 | 3.200e-04 |
| Varshni `beta` (K) | 93.0 | 140.0 | 170.0 |

**The electron mass settles this.** `m_e` is *derived* from the Kane expression rather than
tabulated (aestimo's database lists InAs `m_e = 0.4`, an order of magnitude off), so it is
a consistency check on `Eg`, `Ep`, `delta_so` and `F` together:

| m_e (m0) | InAs | GaSb | InSb |
|---|---|---|---|
| derived with `database` gaps | 0.02493 | 0.03498 | 0.01023 |
| derived with 0 K Varshni gaps | 0.02601 | 0.03901 | 0.01350 |
| the review's own tabulated `m_e` | 0.02600 | 0.03900 | 0.01350 |

With 0 K gaps the derived masses land on the tabulated values essentially exactly; with
the 300 K database gaps InSb comes out 0.0102 against 0.0135, a 24% error. `Ep` and `F` are 0 K quantities,
so **0 K is the self-consistent choice**. This is independent of the Pryor–Pistol
comparison and points the same way.

## Known problems, in priority order

### 1. `a_gap` — RESOLVED. Three errors in the source database, now corrected

The deformation potentials now come from `bandparameters_vurgaftman2001.xlsx`, read from
the review's own tables rather than from aestimo's copy of them. Three database errors:

| | database | review | |
|---|---|---|---|
| `a_v` InAs | +1.00 | -1.00 | **sign flipped** |
| `a_v` InSb | +0.31 | -0.36 | **sign flipped** |
| `a_v` GaSb | -1.32 | -0.80 | right sign, wrong magnitude |
| `a_c` InSb | -6.04 | -6.94 | transposed digit? |
| `a_c` GaSb | -9.33 | -7.50 | |
| gaps | ~300 K | 0 K | see the mass check above |

The review fixes the sign convention explicitly: *"the change in energy
dEg = a(exx+eyy+eyy) must be positive [under compression]. This implies a negative value
for a = a_c + a_v. Note that our sign convention for a_v is different from many other
works found in the literature."* So `a_gap = a_c + a_v`, which this module already
assumed -- the database had simply flipped `a_v` for InAs and InSb. GaSb was the only
correct one, which is the opposite of what `GASB_AV_CONVENTION` was built to fix; that
switch is now deprecated.

| a_gap | InAs | GaSb | InSb |
|---|---|---|---|
| database (old) | -4.08 | -8.01 | -5.73 |
| review (now in force) | **-6.08** | **-8.30** | **-7.30** |
| Pryor 1998 Table I, InAs only | -6.00 | | |

`scripts/insb_pryor_pistol_check.py` back-derived -7.29 eV for InSb from Pryor & Pistol's
Table I well *before* the workbook was consulted; the review gives -7.30. That 10 meV
agreement is what identified the error as parametric rather than numerical, and with it
corrected the well now reproduces their Table I to **0 meV on both band edges**.

### 2. What is still unverified

| parameter | materials | note |
|---|---|---|
| `e14` | all four | piezoelectric. Present in the workbook, but as an **addition to the sheet** — the review's band-parameter tables do not cover piezoelectric constants. And the sign disagrees with Pryor 1998 (below). Still a knob. |
| `eps_R` | all four | static dielectric constant, likewise an addition to the sheet, and a 300 K value used at every temperature. Enters only the piezoelectric potential. |

**The `e14` sign.** The workbook gives `e14` negative for all four binaries; `pryor1998.PRYOR_TABLE_I`, transcribed from the arXiv preprint, gives the same magnitudes positive (GaAs +0.159, InAs +0.045 against -0.160 and -0.045 here).
Zincblende has only one independent piezoelectric constant, so a global sign flip flips
the piezoelectric potential everywhere and swaps which side of the dot each carrier is
pushed toward. It does **not** change `|phi|`, the C4 antisymmetry, or anything computed
with `use_piezo=False`. `pryor1998` is a benchmark fixture reproducing a published figure
and keeps its own values, so the two deliberately disagree; if you compare a piezo result
here against a Pryor figure, check that sign first.

`d` is no longer among these -- the review gives it for all three (InAs -3.6, GaSb -4.7, InSb -4.7 eV), which also corrected the GaSb and InSb guesses of -4.6 and -5.0. Likewise GaSb `C44`.

`d` has exactly zero leverage on a biaxially strained well (no off-diagonal strain), so it
could not have contributed to the benchmark discrepancy. It does matter for dots.

### 3. Other cross-check discrepancies against Pryor 1998 Table I (InAs only)

| parameter | Pryor 1998 | materials_sb | diff |
|---|---|---|---|
| `Eg` | 0.418 | 0.417 | -0.001 |
| `Ep` | 22.200 | 21.500 | -0.700 **check** |
| `gamma1L` | 19.670 | 20.000 | +0.330 **check** |
| `gamma2L` | 8.370 | 8.500 | +0.130 |
| `gamma3L` | 9.290 | 9.200 | -0.090 |
| `delta_so` | 0.380 | 0.390 | +0.010 |
| `b` | -1.800 | -1.800 | +0.000 |
| `d` | -3.600 | -3.600 | +0.000 |
| `a_c` | -6.660 | -5.080 | +1.580 **check** |
| `a_v` | 0.660 | -1.000 | -1.660 **check** |


`a_c` and `a_v` differ a lot individually while their SUM agrees to 80 meV (-6.00 vs -6.08). That is exactly what the review warns about: *"The distribution of the hydrostatic pressure shift
between the conduction and valence bands is generally based from theoretical predictions
in this review."* The split between bands is model-dependent; only `a_gap` is constrained
by experiment. So this row is agreement, not disagreement -- and it is why `a_gap` is the
quantity to check.

`Ep` at -0.7 eV and the Luttingers do differ genuinely between the two sources. They feed
the derived mass and the eight-band Hamiltonian, so they are worth knowing about, but the
0 K derived masses reproduce the review's tabulated values exactly with the numbers in
force here.
